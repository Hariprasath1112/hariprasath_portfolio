Leg 01 / Flight Brief

Project overview

CareerPilot AI is a personal AI career coach that replaces scattered advice — a resume template here, a mock interview there — with one connected system that studies a person's skills and goals, then plots the route to a role that fits.

Vision

To become the world's most trusted AI career companion — helping millions of professionals reach fulfilling careers through personalised, data-driven guidance.

Mission

Democratise access to world-class career guidance by pairing proven career-development methodology with always-on AI.

Business goal

Convert free members into paid Professional/Premium subscribers by proving value inside the first session — a completed assessment and a scored resume.

User goal

Understand where I stand, know exactly what to fix, and walk into interviews — and negotiations — prepared.

Leg 02 / Turbulence Report

The problem

Career decisions are high-stakes and infrequent, so most people make them with the least practice and the worst tools.

User pain

No sense of direction

People can list their skills but can't translate them into a shortlist of roles that actually fit — so they default to whatever job board suggests.

User pain

Resumes built blind

Without ATS feedback, candidates don't know if a bot ever really "reads" their resume before a human does.

Business gap

Fragmented tools

Assessment apps, resume builders and interview-prep apps live in three different tabs with three different logins and no shared profile.

Market gap

Generic advice at scale

Existing platforms optimise for content volume (blog posts, templates) rather than a personalised, data-backed plan.

Opportunity

One connected profile

If assessment, resume, interview and job-match all read from the same profile, every module gets smarter with use.

Opportunity

Confidence, quantified

Turning vague feedback into scores (92/100, 88% match) gives people something concrete to improve — and to trust.

Leg 04 / Discover

Research & insights

Discovery mixed generative research (why people stall in a career search) with market scanning (why existing tools don't close that gap).

241:1 interviews
312Survey responses
6Stakeholder sessions
9Competitors scanned

Finding 01

78% of respondents had used 3+ separate tools for one job search — and lost progress switching between them.

Finding 02

Candidates trusted a numeric score ("88% match") far more than qualitative advice alone, but wanted the "why" behind it.

Finding 03

Interview anxiety was rated the #1 blocker — ahead of resume quality — yet had the fewest dedicated tools.

Leg 17 / Interaction Design

Motion & micro-interactions

Small, purposeful motion reassures rather than decorates — every animation reports status.

Progress fill

The assessment bar eases forward (320ms) on each answer, reinforcing that input was registered.

Score ring

The resume score ring counts up from 0 to 92 on load — a small reveal that rewards patience.

Card hover

Job cards lift 2px with a soft shadow on hover, signalling they're tappable before the cursor reaches "Apply."

Toggle states

Monthly/Annual pricing toggle slides with a spring easing, and prices cross-fade rather than jump.

Live chat pulse

A subtle pulse on the chat bubble indicates "Available 24/7" without an intrusive badge count.

Skeleton loading

Job matches and resume suggestions load in as skeleton rows first, avoiding a blank-screen flash.

Leg 18 / Accessibility

Accessibility

Every score and status is communicated in more than one channel, so colour is never the only signal.

Contrast

Body text and buttons checked to WCAG AA (4.5:1) against both paper and dark surfaces.

Colour-independent status

Match scores pair colour with a ✓ icon and percentage label — never colour alone.

Keyboard flow

Assessment, resume and pricing toggles are fully operable via Tab / Enter / Arrow keys.

Forms

Every input has a persistent label (not placeholder-only) and inline, specific error text.

Leg 19 / Usability Testing

Testing & iteration

Two rounds of moderated testing (8 participants each) on the assessment-to-resume flow.

62%→91%Task success rate
-38%Time to first result
8Participants / round
Before

Users abandoned sign-up because the password field gave no way to check what they'd typed.

After

Added a show/hide eye icon on both password fields — sign-up drop-off fell noticeably in round 2.

Before

Users didn't trust a bare "88% Match" number and skipped straight to "Apply Now."

After

Added a Company Fit breakdown (Skills, Culture, Growth, Compensation) beneath the headline score.

Before

SSO buttons (Google/LinkedIn) sat below the primary form and were rarely noticed.

After

Kept SSO below the fold but added an "Or continue with" divider for clearer scanning — tested equally successful either way.

Leg 22 / Key Learnings

What this project taught me

Numbers build trust faster than words

A visible score moved people to action more reliably than any amount of persuasive copy.

One profile beats one feature

The biggest lift came from connecting modules, not adding new ones.

Anxiety needs a rehearsal space

Interview prep only got used once mock sessions felt low-stakes and repeatable.
