// 01 — Project Overview

One account.
Six disconnected products.

CodingUniverse began as five separately-shipped tools — courses, an IDE, a forum, a jobs board, and an AI chatbot bolted on last. Version 2.0 asked a simple question: what if it felt like one platform that actually got you hired?

Product vision

Become the operating system for a developer's career — the single place where someone learns a skill, proves it, and gets paid for it.

Product mission

Remove every barrier between "I want to learn to code" and "I got the job" — geography, cost, or a confusing product experience.

Business goal

Lift free-to-Pro conversion and reduce churn by making the paid tier feel indispensable rather than a paywall.

User goal

Always know the next right step — the next lesson, the next challenge, the next milestone toward a real job.

Key features in scope

🧠 AI-Powered Tutor

Adaptive 24/7 mentor that explains, debugs, and drills based on skill gaps.

💻 Live Playground

Browser IDE, 30+ languages, real-time collaboration and instant feedback.

🗺️ Career Roadmaps

Phased, milestone-tracked paths from beginner to job-ready specialist.

🏆 Coding Arena

Gamified challenges, XP, streaks, and a competitive leaderboard.

👥 Community

500K-strong forum for questions, showcases, and peer mentorship.

💼 Career Center

Curated jobs, resume tools, salary data, and mentorship booking.

// 02 — Problem Statement

Learners weren't finishing.
Not because content was bad — because direction was missing.

USER PAIN

"What do I even do next?"

No connective tissue between courses, practice, and career tools — every session started from zero.

BUSINESS

Flat Pro conversion

Free tier felt complete enough; the value of upgrading wasn't visible in the moment of struggle.

MARKET GAP

Content vs. outcome

Competitors sell courses. Nobody was selling a credible, trackable path to a hired outcome.

OPPORTUNITY

AI as a guide, not a gimmick

An AI tutor that's contextual to a learner's actual roadmap — not a generic chatbot bolted on the side.

"I'd finish a course, feel good for a day, then have no idea what to build or learn next — so I'd just stop." — Research participant, career-switcher cohort

04_define.md
// 06 — Define

Turning insight into direction.

Problem Definition

Developers on CodingUniverse have plenty of content, but no single system tells them what to do next, whether they're actually improving, or how close they are to being hired.

How Might We…

  • …surface a single "next step" at all times, regardless of which tool a learner is in?
  • …make the AI tutor feel like it knows the learner's actual roadmap, not generic Q&A?
  • …turn progress into a visible, motivating signal (streaks, XP, skill %) without feeling gamey?
  • …connect skill mastery directly to real job opportunities?

Goals

User: Never wonder what to do next. Always see visible proof of progress.

Business: Increase D30 retention and free-to-Pro conversion by making the paid tier the obvious "unlock" at the moment of friction.

// 17 — Interaction Design

Small moments, deliberately built.

⚡ Button lift

4px translateY + shadow bloom on hover, 120ms ease-out — confirms interactivity without feeling bouncy.

📊 Progress fill

Skill bars animate on scroll-into-view, easing from 0 to value over 600ms.

🔥 Streak pulse

The flame icon gently pulses when a streak is at risk of breaking — urgency without alarm.

💬 AI typing dots

Three-dot typing indicator mirrors familiar chat UX so the AI Tutor feels alive, not robotic.

// 18 — Accessibility

Built to WCAG 2.1 AA, by default.

Contrast

All text-on-color pairings verified ≥4.5:1; mint-on-white CTA text uses white, never ink, to stay compliant.

Keyboard navigation

Full tab-order audit across Playground and AI Tutor — the two most interaction-dense surfaces.

Focus states

2px visible focus ring in violet on every interactive element, never suppressed for aesthetics.

// 19 — Prototype, Testing & Iteration

What broke in round one.

Method

Moderated remote sessions, 45 min, think-aloud protocol on a clickable end-to-end Figma prototype.

Participants

14 learners across both personas, recruited from the existing free-tier user base.

Task success

86%

completed the "find your next step" task unassisted — up from 41% on the legacy product.

V1

"Next Up" buried in a side panel

4 of 14 participants never noticed it existed during their first task.

V2 — Shipped

Promoted to a hero card on Dashboard

Full-width, top-of-page placement with a single primary CTA — visibility jumped to 13/14.

// 22 — Key Learnings

What I'd carry into the next system.

01. Direction beats density

Adding one clear "next step" moved metrics further than any new content library did.

02. AI needs context to earn trust

The tutor's usage tripled the moment it became aware of a learner's actual roadmap, not just generic Q&A.

03. Test placement, not just visuals

The biggest usability win came from moving a component, not restyling it.
