01 — Overview

What is
GreenLife?

GreenLife is a comprehensive plant education web platform that helps users discover flowers, trees, grass types, and leaves — while learning about their incredible health and ecological benefits. The platform bridges the gap between botanical knowledge and everyday wellbeing.

🌸

Flower Directory

Detailed profiles of 10 popular flowers — Rose, Lavender, Sunflower, Tulip, and more — with health benefits, care guides, and Tamil name references for local audiences.

🌳

Tree Encyclopedia

In-depth guides to 5 key tree species including Oak, Maple, Pine, Willow, and Bamboo, covering ecological impact, environmental benefits, and fascinating facts.

🌿

Leaves & Grass Types

Educational sections explaining photosynthesis, transpiration, and the science of leaves, alongside a guide to Kentucky Bluegrass, Bermuda, Fescue, and Zoysia varieties.

💚

Health Benefits Hub

A dedicated section backed by scientific studies showing how plants improve physical health, mental wellbeing, air quality, and provide medicinal value.

02 — Problem Statement

The Gap in
Plant Knowledge

Most people are aware that plants are beneficial — but few know exactly how, why, or which plants serve what purpose. Existing resources are either too scientific and inaccessible, or too shallow to be truly useful. There was no single, beautifully designed destination for everyday plant education.



GreenLife set out to change that — creating a warm, approachable, visually engaging platform where curiosity about nature is met with clarity and depth.

03 — Research & Discovery

Understanding
Our Users

Before designing a single screen, we conducted user research to understand what people actually want from a plant education platform — and what frustrates them about existing ones.

01 · Finding

People Want Context, Not Just Facts

Users don't just want to know that Rose has vitamin C — they want to understand how to use it, when it matters, and why it works. Context drives retention.

🔍
02 · Finding

Bilingual Access Matters

For Tamil-speaking audiences, seeing familiar names like "மல்லிகை" alongside "Jasmine" creates immediate trust and emotional connection with the content.

🌐
03 · Finding

Visual Hierarchy Drives Exploration

Users naturally gravitate toward well-structured pages with clear tabs (Health Benefits, Medical Uses, Care Guide, Fun Facts) rather than scrolling through walls of text.

👁️
04 · Finding

Color Filtering Increases Engagement

Color variant selectors for flowers (Red, Pink, White, Yellow) help users connect the content to what they see in the real world, making the experience feel personal.

🎨
05 · Finding

Nature = Trust & Calm

Green-dominant palettes with natural photography build immediate subconscious trust. Users rated green-themed designs as more credible for nature-related content.

🌱
06 · Finding

Consistent Navigation is Critical

Users expect consistent access to all sections (Flowers, Trees, Grass, Leaves, Health) regardless of which page they're on — quick links in footers are heavily used.

🗺️

05 — Design Goals

What We Set
Out to Solve

Six clear design objectives guided every decision — from layout structure to color choices to content hierarchy.

01

Make botanical knowledge approachable

Present complex plant science in plain, warm language that feels educational without being intimidating — suitable for curious students and lifelong learners alike.

02

Support bilingual Tamil-English audience

Prominently feature Tamil flower and tree names to create cultural resonance and immediate recognition for local South Indian users.

03

Build a consistent, navigable information architecture

Every page maintains the same navigation and footer Quick Links, ensuring zero dead ends and always-available access to all six main sections.

04

Use colour as a content tool, not just aesthetics

Color variant selectors on flower pages (Red, Pink, White, Purple) serve both UX functionality and the delight of visual exploration.

05

Build trust through scientific grounding

The Health Benefits section uses documented research statistics — hospital recovery rates, productivity studies, air purification data — to establish credibility.

06

Design for exploration and serendipitous discovery

Tabbed content (Health Benefits / Medical Uses / Care Guide / Fun Facts) encourages users to linger and discover beyond their initial search intent.

10 — Key Features

Design
Decisions

Every major UI pattern in GreenLife was a deliberate choice rooted in user research findings and accessibility principles.

🎨

Color Variant Selectors

Clickable color dot selectors on flower pages let users filter by flower colour — Red, Pink, White, Yellow, Purple — making plant identification tactile and immediate.

📑

Tabbed Content Architecture

Each plant page offers four distinct tabs — Health Benefits, Medical Uses, Care Guide, and Fun Facts — allowing users to self-direct their exploration depth.

🌐

Tamil-English Bilingual Labels

All 10 flowers and 5 trees display their Tamil names in parentheses — building immediate cultural recognition for local South Indian users.

🧭

Persistent Navigation & Quick Links

Top navigation and footer Quick Links appear on every single page, ensuring the site never creates navigation dead ends regardless of entry point.

📊

Data-Driven Ecological Stats

Tree pages feature real ecological impact numbers — "90,000 acorns in a lifetime," "48 pounds of CO₂ per year" — making abstract data viscerally meaningful.

🔬

Scientific Evidence Section

The Health Benefits page presents clinical study data in plain English, establishing GreenLife's authority and differentiating it from superficial plant blogs.

12 — Key Learnings

What This
Project Taught

Building GreenLife surfaced important lessons about designing educational content platforms with cultural nuance.

🌱

Cultural language builds emotional trust

Adding Tamil plant names wasn't just an accessibility feature — it fundamentally changed how local users felt about the platform. Language is intimacy.

📐

Flat architecture beats deep navigation trees

Keeping everything accessible within two clicks reduced bounce rates and encouraged users to explore beyond their initial intent.

🎯

Specificity beats generality in health content

"Reduces anxiety" is forgettable. "Rose tea can help reduce anxiety and improve mood" is actionable. Specific claims drive return visits.

🎨

Green isn't one colour — it's a whole language

The 10-step green scale from #0B2614 to #F0FFF4 gave the design system enormous expressive range while maintaining total visual coherence.
